I completed my undergraduate studies at the University of California, Berkeley. I studied computer science, data science, and history, with a minor in digital humanities. My data science concentration was in geospatial information and technology, and my history concentration was in the history of science. At Berkeley, I was very fortunate to be advised by [Sarah Chasins](https://schasins.com/) in [PLAIT Lab](https://plait-lab.org/). I am grateful for all the kind people in PLAIT who inspired me to get into research.

For some fun additional background, I wrote my history thesis on the trajectory of IBM's industrial research labs in the 1990s. Amongst other things, I also wrote a research paper on [numerical taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_taxonomy) and essentialism in the mid-20th century. I am especially grateful to Ari Edmundson and Jordan Mursinna for helping me be a better (albeit still pretty bad) student of history. I like the humanities :\). 

While teaching at Berkeley, I was involved in many cool things. In Data 100, I helped create a couple of new assignments and led the creation of its first online exams. I also had the pleasure of working on the [Human Contexts and Ethics](https://cdss.berkeley.edu/dsus/human-contexts-and-ethics) team (many thanks to the excellent guidance from Ari and [Lisa Yan](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Faculty/Homepages/yanlisa.html)!), where I helped create course content that integrated real data ethics considerations into various data science courses. In my free time, I enjoy cooking, baking, and casually playing video games.
